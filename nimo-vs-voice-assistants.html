What Nimo adds that an assistant doesn't

  • A presence. An assistant waits for a wake word. Nimo is there when you sit down or get in, notices when you leave or arrive, and has a small life of his own in between.
  • Emotion. He's happy to see you, patient in traffic, sleepy late at night. It sounds small, and it changes how a long day feels.
  • Signals instead of screens. His eyes carry the message, so there's nothing to read. You get it in a glance, like a friend's smile.
  • Play. Tap his head for a quick game on your break or when you're parked.

What an assistant does better

Be honest about what you need. For turn-by-turn directions, hands-free calls, dictated replies, timers and controlling the lights, your assistant does it brilliantly, and Nimo isn't trying to replace it. Keep CarPlay, Android Auto or your smart speaker.

If what you want is for the workday or the drive to feel less lonely, that's a gap no assistant fills, and it's the gap Nimo was made for.

So which should you use?

Most people will end up with both: an assistant for tasks, and Nimo for company. They don't compete, they cover different jobs.

Questions

Companions and assistants

Does Nimo replace Siri, Alexa or Google?
No. Keep using them for tasks like calls, timers, maps and smart home control. Nimo is designed to sit alongside them as a companion, with a face, moods and small moments of his own.
Is Nimo a voice assistant?
Not mainly. He's a companion first: a character with moods who reacts to your day. He uses his voice for short, useful moments, such as a hello, a message read aloud or a break reminder.
Does Nimo work with CarPlay or Android Auto?
Nimo is designed to sit on the dashboard next to your phone or car screen, not inside them. Keep your map app for directions; Nimo is there for company and glanceable nudges.
Can Nimo sit next to a smart speaker on my desk?
Yes. A smart speaker handles music, timers and the smart home; Nimo keeps you company, nudges you to take breaks and plays a quick game when you need one.
