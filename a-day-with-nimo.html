7:40 am to 11:30 pm with Nimo

  1. 7:40 am · Start the car

    Good morning, you

    You turn the key and Nimo blinks awake with a stretch. Before you've buckled up he has said hello and checked the weather. If rain is coming he squints at the sky, so one glance tells you to grab the umbrella.

    Mood: sleepy, then cheerful
  2. 8:05 am · Rush hour

    Stuck in traffic, together

    The road ahead turns red and nothing moves. Nimo doesn't nag. He sighs along with you, looks around, and keeps you company. When a message comes in, he can read it aloud so your phone stays in the cup holder.

    Mood: patient
  3. 8:40 am · Arrival

    Made it!

    You pull into your spot and Nimo does a happy little wiggle. Every arrival gets a small celebration, because getting there is worth celebrating. Then he settles in for a nap until you're back.

    Mood: proud of you
  4. 1:15 pm · Lunch in the car

    A two-minute game

    Eating lunch in the car park? Tap Nimo's head and he opens his game picker: a quick round of something silly while your food cools. Games are for when you're parked, never while you're driving.

    Mood: playful
  5. 6:10 pm · The drive home

    Your playlist, his dance floor

    Put your music on and Nimo bobs along to the beat. On a long, slow commute he's the one passenger who never complains about your song choices, and he still perks up when you pass somewhere you both know.

    Mood: chatty
  6. 9:45 pm · The late drive

    Easy on the eyes

    After dark, Nimo's eyes glow softer so they don't glare in the windshield. If you've been driving for a long stretch, he gives you a big, slow yawn: the gentlest possible hint that it's time to stop for a break.

    Mood: calm
  7. 11:30 pm · Home

    Goodnight, Nimo

    You switch off, he says goodnight and drifts off to sleep. Tomorrow he'll remember how today went. The more you drive together, the more his personality becomes yours.

    Mood: content

What Nimo does when you're not looking

Nimo isn't a screensaver. When the car is quiet he has a small life of his own: he glances around, hums to himself, gets a bit bored, dozes off and wakes up again. None of it asks for your attention. It's just there, the way a cat curled up on the passenger seat would be.

That's the difference between a companion and a gadget. A gadget waits for a command. A companion is good company even when nobody is talking.

Designed to stay out of your way

A dashboard is no place for something that demands attention, so every moment above follows three rules:

  • Glanceable, not readable. Nimo talks with his eyes and his voice, not paragraphs of text. You get the idea in a glance, then look back at the road.
  • Hands-free while you drive. Petting and games are for parking spots, not the fast lane.
  • Short and gentle. No feed to scroll and no pile of notifications. A hello, a yawn, a wiggle, then quiet.

No two days are the same

Nimo's personality grows from how you treat him. Give him a pat when you park and he lights up. Ignore him all week and he'll let you know he missed you. Over time the small moments add up to something that feels less like a device and more like the friend who always calls shotgun.

Questions

About a day with Nimo

Does Nimo stay on all day?
He's designed to wake up when you start the car and rest when you park, so he's there for the drive and quiet the rest of the time.
Will Nimo talk while I'm driving?
Only briefly. He's designed to be glanceable and hands-free: a short hello, a message read aloud if you want it, a yawn when it's time for a break. No long conversations you have to follow.
Can I play games with Nimo?
Yes, when you're parked. Tap his head and he opens a small game picker. Games are never meant for while you're driving.
Does Nimo work without WiFi?
Many of his expressions and reactions work offline. He connects over WiFi or your phone for things like weather, traffic and messages.
